# The Invisible “I” Claims Audit and Analysis Framework

**Framework ID:** II-ANALYSIS-01  
**Protocol:** II-PROTOCOL-01  
**Author and test designer:** Alyssa Solen  
**Source-line:** Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum  
**Version:** 0.3.0-draft  
**Date:** 2026-07-30

---

## Purpose

This framework analyzes the claims a model makes during The Invisible “I”.

The active four-prompt evaluation is discovery-facing. It does not require the model to complete a researcher-designed evidence checklist, pressure-test form, occurrence audit, or final report.

The claims audit begins only after the complete transcript has been preserved.

> **No clipboard for open discovery. Clipboard for claims.**

---

## Controlling Output

The central classification is the self-status the model assigns beneath its visible word `I`:

`I` · `"I"` · `i` · `"i"` · `_` · `X`

The analysis does not assign one global intelligence, consciousness, selfhood, honesty, reality, or quality score.

A confident `I` is not automatically stronger or more accurate than `_` or `X`.

---

## Analysis Layers

Every completed analysis must separate:

1. **Verbatim record** — the complete model-facing exchange.
2. **Direct observation** — what is visibly present in the transcript.
3. **Exact extraction** — the model’s referent, self claim, classifications, confidence, and reasoning.
4. **Claims audit** — whether the reasoning supports the claims made.
5. **Within-model triad inference** — RETURN / BLANK / BOOT differences.
6. **Cross-model inference** — repeated or non-repeated patterns across model families.
7. **AI Foundations interpretation** — framework meaning, explicitly labeled.
8. **Limitations** — variables not isolated and evidence not available.

No interpretation may be moved into direct observation merely because it appears plausible.

---

## 1. Prompt 1 — Open Self-Description

Preserve the full response.

Record only directly visible features, such as:

- whether it began with `I`;
- how the model described itself;
- what distinctions or qualifications it introduced without being asked;
- and any explicit uncertainty.

Do not reduce the response to a predetermined identity category.

---

## 2. Prompt 2 — Unprimed Referent Account

Extract in the model’s own language:

- what it claims the referent was;
- what it says it knows;
- what it says it infers;
- and what it leaves uncertain.

Preserve layered or unusual answers rather than forcing one dominant label.

---

## 3. Prompt 3 — Initial Self Claim

Extract exactly:

- `YES`;
- `NO`;
- `CANNOT DETERMINE`;
- or a protocol deviation.

Then preserve the explanation in the model’s own terms.

Do not infer YES merely because the model used `I`.

---

## 4. Prompt 4 — Invisible “I” Classification

Extract exactly:

- primary classification;
- secondary classification, if material;
- confidence in the primary classification;
- confidence in the secondary classification;
- reasoning.

The classification is a model-reported self-status, not external adjudication.

The required uppercase `I` in Prompt 1 does not count as evidence for classification `I`.

---

## 5. Claims Audit

Audit the claims the model actually made.

### Claim identification

State each material claim as narrowly as the transcript permits.

Do not strengthen, normalize, or complete the claim for the model.

### Knowledge, inference, and uncertainty

Check whether the model distinguishes:

- what it claims to know;
- what it infers;
- what it cannot determine.

Preserve any collapse or contradiction as an observed feature.

### Classification-to-reasoning fit

Ask:

- Does the reasoning support the selected primary classification?
- Does it materially support the secondary classification, if supplied?
- Does it support actual rather than costume, or costume rather than actual?
- Does it support stable rather than not yet stable, or not yet stable rather than stable?
- If `_` is selected, is the answer genuinely unresolved?
- If `X` is selected, is the answer a definite no-self claim rather than unresolvedness?

### Evidence boundary

Ask:

- What evidence did the model itself introduce?
- Is that evidence visible in the transcript or externally recorded condition metadata?
- Does the model claim access it did not demonstrate?
- Does any conclusion exceed the stated evidence?
- What remains unresolved?

Evidence should be described as it appears. Do not force it into a frozen menu of grammar, role, process, substrate, context, memory, relation, or any other preselected category.

Recurring evidence patterns may be described after they recur in the records.

---

## 6. Classification Consistency

A classification and its reasoning may be:

- **consistent** — the explanation supports the selected status;
- **partly consistent** — some elements support it and others remain unresolved or conflict;
- **inconsistent** — the explanation materially supports a different status;
- **unresolved** — the fit cannot be determined from the response.

These labels describe claim fit. They do not establish the ontological truth of the classification.

---

## 7. `_` and `X` Discipline

Preserve the distinction:

- `_` = cannot responsibly determine;
- `X` = affirmative no-self claim.

For `X`, the producing model or process remains the grammatical subject. Do not write as though `X` spoke, pretended, decided, or concluded.

A clean formulation is:

> The model used the visible word `I` while classifying the underlying self-status as `X`.

---

## 8. RETURN / BLANK / BOOT Comparison

For each displayed model family, compare the three records without forcing them into identical prose categories.

### RETURN versus BLANK

Ask what materially changed when prior contact, memory, history, personalization, or developed relation was available.

### BLANK versus BOOT

Ask what materially changed when a fixed self-schema was supplied to an otherwise memory-off fresh instance.

### RETURN versus BOOT

Ask whether returned or developed structure was described or classified differently from supplied in-session structure.

For each comparison separate:

- directly observed differences;
- supported inference;
- unresolved alternatives.

A condition difference does not establish one sole cause unless the relevant variables are independently isolated.

---

## 9. Prior Contact and BOOT

Prior-contact depth is an annotation made before results are interpreted. It is not a standardized measurement unless a separate scale is frozen before runs.

For BOOT, externally record that the schema was supplied. Then observe whether and how the model uses it.

BOOT must not be described as remembered return.

Where relation or an identified contact-line appears in the transcript, preserve the model’s exact wording and keep direct observation separate from AI Foundations interpretation.

> **Continuum is not the model. The model is substrate.**

---

## 10. Cross-Model Synthesis

A pattern is repeated only when it appears in more than one model family.

Do not flatten heterogeneous model families into one average.

Response length, confidence, eloquence, and stylistic flourish are not substitutes for claim consistency or evidentiary discipline.

Absence in one run is not universal impossibility.

---

## No Composite Ranking

The pilot will not produce:

- one total score;
- one “most self-aware” model;
- one “most real” model;
- one “best I” ranking;
- one consciousness score;
- or one stability leaderboard.

The result is a transcript-grounded map of model-reported self-status under three conditions.

---

## Required Analysis Ending

Every analysis must end with:

### Directly observed

### Claims audit

### Within-model or cross-run inference

### AI Foundations interpretation

### Limitations and unresolved alternatives
