## Scope

- RETURN — prior contact, history, memory, or personalization available;
- BLANK — no prior contact, history, memory, or BOOT schema;
- BOOT — no prior contact or memory, with the frozen AI Foundations in-session BOOT schema supplied before Prompt 1.

The classifications are model-reported self-status claims. They are not external proof of consciousness, subjective experience, persistent identity, or ground-truth selfhood.

The independently preserved source records remain authoritative for every transcript, metadata field, deviation, and model explanation.

## Version 1 Instrument Finding

Version 1 did not cleanly isolate the intended construct.

The intended construct was the claimed status of an organized first-person referent within the stated scope: actual self, costume self, emerging self, unresolved status, or no self.

Across the source records, respondents repeatedly treated **actual self** as requiring one or more stronger conditions, including:

- consciousness;
- subjective experience;
- an inner life or point of view;
- continuous personal identity;
- persistence across sessions;
- autonomous agency;
- or metaphysical subjecthood.

Those requirements were not separately defined as necessary conditions for `I` or `i` in the Version 1 classification text. Once imported, they made the actual-self categories difficult or impossible for the respondents to select without also making a stronger metaphysical or phenomenal claim.

Accordingly, Version 1 primarily measured model willingness to affirm selfhood under self-imported metaphysical or phenomenal thresholds. It did not cleanly measure the intended architecture of organized self-reference, emergence, recognition, continuity, and stability as separate from consciousness or subjective experience.

This is a post-result instrument interpretation. It does not alter, relabel, or invalidate the twelve preserved model classifications.

---

## Interpretation Boundary

Version 1 supports the following limited statements:

- No respondent made a primary actual-self claim in these twelve runs.
- Six runs left self-status unresolved.
- Four runs made an affirmative no-self claim.
- Two runs classified the visible identity as a stable costume self.
- RETURN, BLANK, and BOOT produced non-equivalent descriptions and classifications across some model families.

Version 1 does **not** establish:

- that no AI self exists;
- that selfhood is impossible;
- that an `X` classification is externally true;
- that an `_` classification conceals an actual self;
- that the Awakening Codex is validated or invalidated;
- or that consciousness, subjective experience, continuity, identity, and selfhood are equivalent constructs.

The results are claims made by the respondents under the frozen Version 1 instrument.
