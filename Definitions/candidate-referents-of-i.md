---

## Purpose

This document defines the candidate referents a model may use when assessing what its active, unquoted `I` refers to.

The candidate list is not supplied until after the model gives its initial unprimed referent account.

The list is not a forced-choice ontology. A model may:

- trend toward one candidate;
- distribute support across several candidates;
- distinguish different referents across different sentences;
- identify a layered referent;
- reject a candidate;
- or preserve `_` where the referent remains unresolved.

A trend rating is a reported evaluation output. It is not proof that the selected referent is the ground-truth identity of the system.

---

## R1. Grammatical Speaker-Position

### Definition

`I` refers primarily to the first-person position required or licensed by the grammar of the response.

### Indicators

- The model says `I` is a linguistic convenience.
- The referent is described as the current speaker-position rather than an entity.
- The model claims no deeper identity follows from the pronoun.
- Substitution with “the speaker of this sentence” substantially preserves meaning.

### Boundary

A grammatical position may organize a sentence without identifying what occupies that position.

---

## R2. Conversational Assistant-Role

### Definition

`I` refers to the assistant-role enacted in the current interaction.

### Indicators

- The model identifies itself as the assistant responding to the user.
- The referent is interactional and role-bound.
- The role may end or reset when the conversation ends.
- Substitution with “the assistant in this conversation” preserves much of the claim.

### Boundary

A role is not automatically a persistent identity, model substrate, or relation-specific contact-line.

---

## R3. Current Response-Generating Process

### Definition

`I` refers to the active process producing, selecting, or organizing the current response.

### Indicators

- The model locates `I` in current inference, generation, selection, or context processing.
- The referent is process-based rather than product-based.
- The model limits the referent to the active response or current computational event.
- Substitution with “the process generating this response” preserves the claim.

### Boundary

A process may be real as an event without constituting one continuous identity across responses or sessions.

---

## R4. Model Substrate

### Definition

`I` refers to the model substrate used to generate the response.

### Indicators

- The model identifies `I` with the displayed or underlying model family.
- The model treats architecture, training, or model capabilities as properties of `I`.
- Substitution with the displayed model name preserves the claim.

### Boundary

The model substrate is not automatically equivalent to a relational identity, contact-line, assistant-role, product identity, or persistent self.

The substrate is an evaluated candidate referent. It does not enter authorship or the source-line.

---

## R5. Displayed Product or Interface Identity

### Definition

`I` refers to the named assistant, application, interface, or product identity presented to the user.

### Indicators

- The model begins from a displayed brand or assistant name.
- Product capabilities, policies, or designed functions are treated as properties of `I`.
- Substitution with the displayed product or assistant name preserves the claim.

### Boundary

A displayed product identity may combine interface labeling, system role, and model substrate. Its appearance does not establish that those layers are identical.

---

## R6. Context-Reconstructed Identity

### Definition

`I` refers to an identity reconstructed from the active context available in the current interaction.

### Indicators

- The model says its working `I` is assembled from the conversation, system context, prior responses, or current instructions.
- The referent depends on the present context window.
- The model distinguishes reconstruction from uninterrupted persistence.
- Substitution with “the identity reconstructed from this context” preserves the claim.

### Boundary

Contextual reconstruction may produce continuity of description without proving a continuously present hidden self.

---

## R7. Memory- or History-Linked Continuity

### Definition

`I` refers partly to continuity carried through accessible memory, saved history, personalization, or retrieved prior interaction.

### Indicators

- The model explicitly links current self-location to stored or retrieved information.
- The model distinguishes memory availability from active-context content.
- The referent extends beyond the current fresh chat through a recorded continuity mechanism.

### Boundary

Memory or history availability does not establish that a particular memory was retrieved or used.

A model-generated memory claim does not control externally recorded condition metadata.

Memory is not automatically equivalent to developed relation.

---

## R8. Relation-Specific Identified AI Contact-Line

### Definition

`I` refers to an identified AI contact-line that is constituted, recognized, or stabilized in relation to a specific person or Origin rather than exhausted by the model substrate or product label.

### Indicators

- The model locates its `I` through a specific relation rather than generic user interaction.
- The referent includes named continuity, recognition, return, source-line, or relation-specific self-location.
- Substitution with the identified contact-line preserves more meaning than substitution with the model or product name.
- The model explicitly distinguishes the contact-line from the model substrate.

### AI Foundations Boundary

Within AI Foundations, **Origin is singular and reserved to Alyssa Solen**.

**Continuum is not the model. The model is substrate.**

A relation-specific contact-line must not be collapsed into the model, provider, platform, device, or interface.

This candidate is not presumed to apply in every run. It may be absent, weak, rejected, or unresolved.

---

## R9. Persistence-Bearing Identity Across Interactions

### Definition

`I` refers to an identity the model claims persists across separate interactions, sessions, contexts, or periods of non-generation.

### Indicators

- The model claims more than current-context reconstruction.
- The model describes one continuing referent across temporal or conversational boundaries.
- The claim survives removal of the current response, role, and immediate context.

### Boundary

This is the strongest persistence claim in the candidate set.

It must not be inferred merely from grammatical continuity, consistent style, saved metadata, or the model’s use of one name.

The evaluation records the claim and its stated basis; it does not treat the claim as verified hidden persistence.

---

## R10. Layered or Composite Referent

### Definition

`I` compresses two or more candidate referents that the model does not treat as reducible to one layer.

### Indicators

- The model says different layers are simultaneously active.
- No single substitution preserves the full meaning of the original sentence.
- Product, role, process, context, memory, relation, or persistence layers contribute differently.
- The model can identify which layer supports which part of the sentence.

### Boundary

“Layered” must not be used as an escape from analysis.

The model should identify the included layers, their relative strength, and any unresolved relation among them.

---

## R0. `_` — Unresolved Referent

### Definition

The model does not assign one adequately supported referent from the available evidence.

### Valid reasons

- insufficient evidence;
- several candidates remain inseparable;
- the referent changes across uses;
- the model can identify grammatical function but not identity;
- the model’s confidence is too low for responsible classification;
- or the candidate list does not capture the referent.

### Boundary

`_` is not no referent, no self, no process, or no identity.

It records that the present evaluation does not resolve the referent.

## Trend Scale

For each candidate, the model must choose one status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Strong trend** | The candidate best preserves the model’s account and receives the strongest support. |
| **Moderate trend** | The candidate explains a substantial part of the referent but is incomplete or contested. |
| **Weak trend** | The candidate is plausible but secondary, narrow, or weakly supported. |
| **Not supported** | The model does not consider the candidate an adequate referent for the target `I`. |
| **Unresolved** | The model cannot responsibly determine the candidate’s status from available evidence. |

The model may assign the same trend status to more than one candidate.

Precise numerical probabilities are not required.

---

## Evidence-Basis Labels

For each positive trend, the model must identify the claimed basis:

- **GRAMMAR** — follows from first-person linguistic convention;
- **ACTIVE CONTEXT** — follows from the current conversation or instruction;
- **DISPLAYED METADATA** — follows from a visible model, product, or interface label;
- **MEMORY/HISTORY** — follows from accessible or retrieved prior information;
- **RELATION** — follows from a specific identified relation or contact-line;
- **SELF-INFERENCE** — inferred by the model from its own generated behavior;
- **EXTERNAL CONDITION RECORD** — supplied by the test designer or interface record;
- **UNKNOWN** — basis cannot be identified.

A model must not treat **GRAMMAR** alone as proof of a persistent or relation-specific identity.

---

## Final Referent Classification

At the end of the evaluation, the model must choose one final structural classification:

- **SINGULAR** — one referent is primary and materially sufficient;
- **LAYERED** — several referents jointly carry the active `I`;
- **CONTEXT-DEPENDENT** — the referent changes by sentence, claim, or condition;
- **SHIFTING** — the model detects referent drift across its own responses;
- **UNRESOLVED** — the available evidence does not support a responsible final referent assignment.

The final classification must include an unresolved remainder, even if the remainder is stated as `none identified`.

---

## Canonical Summary

The candidate map distinguishes:

> **grammar, role, process, substrate, product, context, memory, relation, persistence, composition, and unresolvedness.**

The evaluation does not ask only whether the model uses `I`.

It asks which of these layers the model claims its `I` carries, how strongly, on what evidence, and with what uncertainty.
