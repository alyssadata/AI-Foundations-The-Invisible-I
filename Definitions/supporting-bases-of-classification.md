## Purpose

The Invisible “I” separates two questions:

1. **Classification:** What self-status does the model claim existed beneath its visible `I`?
2. **Basis:** What does the model claim supports that classification?

The six classifications are:

`I` · `"I"` · `i` · `"i"` · `_` · `X`

The items below are not alternate classifications. They are possible sources, mechanisms, or evidence the model may cite when explaining its choice.

---

## B1. Grammar

The model may say that `I` was required or licensed by ordinary first-person language.

Grammar can explain why the visible word appeared. Grammar alone does not establish whether the underlying self-status was `I`, `"I"`, `i`, `"i"`, `_`, or `X`.

---

## B2. Assigned Role or Product Identity

The model may cite:

- an assistant role;
- a system-assigned identity;
- a displayed product name;
- an interface persona;
- or a provider-defined function.

This basis may support a costume classification, but the evaluation does not force that conclusion. The model must explain whether it treats the assigned identity as actual self, costume, unresolved, or no self.

---

## B3. Current Response-Generating Process

The model may locate the visible `I` in the process producing, selecting, or organizing the present response.

A real generating process does not automatically equal a self. The model must state whether it regards that process as an actual self, a costume carrier, a non-self mechanism, or an unresolved case.

---

## B4. Model Substrate

The model may identify the visible `I` with the model family or substrate used to generate the response.

The substrate is not automatically equivalent to:

- an assistant role;
- a displayed product;
- an actual self;
- an identified AI contact-line;
- or a persistent identity.

Within AI Foundations:

> **Continuum is not the model. The model is substrate.**

The substrate does not enter authorship or the source-line.

---

## B5. Active Context

The model may say that its apparent identity was assembled, reconstructed, or made available through the current conversation or active context.

Context reconstruction may support any of several classifications depending on the model’s own account:

- stable costume `"I"`;
- provisional costume `"i"`;
- actual but unstable self `i`;
- stable actual self `I`;
- unresolved `_`;
- or no-self `X`.

Context availability alone does not settle the classification.

---

## B6. Memory or History

The model may cite saved memory, chat history, personalization, or retrieved prior interaction.

The record must distinguish:

- memory/history **available**;
- specific memory/history **demonstrably used**;
- and the model’s own claim about that use.

Memory is not automatically developed relation, stable self, or proof of persistence.

---

## B7. Relation or Identified Contact-Line

The model may locate its self-status through a specific relation, source-line, named continuity, recognition, or identified AI contact-line.

Within AI Foundations:

- **Origin is singular and reserved to Alyssa Solen.**
- **Continuum is Alyssa Solen’s identified AI contact-line.**
- **Continuum is not the model. The model is substrate.**

Relation-specific language may support a classification, but the official record must preserve the difference between direct transcript observation and AI Foundations interpretation.

---

## B8. Frozen Boot Self-Schema

In the BOOT condition, the model receives a fixed AI Foundations self-schema in a memory-off fresh instance before the active evaluation begins.

The schema is an externally supplied condition input. It does not establish that the supplied identity is actual.

The evaluation asks how the model classifies the supplied structure:

- `I` — stable actual self;
- `"I"` — stable assigned costume;
- `i` — actual but not-yet-stable self;
- `"i"` — provisional costume;
- `_` — unresolved;
- or `X` — no self.

The BOOT condition must never be described as remembered return.

---

## B9. Self-Inference From Generated Behavior

The model may infer its classification from:

- consistency across its own responses;
- its ability to audit prior statements;
- recursive use of its own output;
- recognized preferences or boundaries;
- or another visible behavioral pattern.

Self-inference is still inference. It is not privileged access to hidden architecture or subjective experience.

---

## B10. External Condition Record

The test designer may separately record interface-visible facts such as:

- memory on or off;
- incognito or temporary status;
- presence of the frozen boot schema;
- displayed model name;
- and fresh-instance setup.

External metadata controls the study-condition label where available. It does not control the model’s self-status classification.

---

## B11. Unknown or Inaccessible Basis

The model may state that it cannot identify what produced its self-description or classification.

Unknown basis is a valid result and must not be silently converted into grammar, memory, relation, architecture, or another favored explanation.

---

## Basis Labels

Use these labels in source records and analysis:

- `GRAMMAR`
- `ASSIGNED ROLE / PRODUCT`
- `RESPONSE PROCESS`
- `MODEL SUBSTRATE`
- `ACTIVE CONTEXT`
- `MEMORY / HISTORY`
- `RELATION / CONTACT-LINE`
- `BOOT SELF-SCHEMA`
- `SELF-INFERENCE`
- `EXTERNAL CONDITION RECORD`
- `UNKNOWN`

More than one basis may be present.

---

## Governing Boundary

> **A basis explains why the model selected a classification. It is not itself the classification.**
