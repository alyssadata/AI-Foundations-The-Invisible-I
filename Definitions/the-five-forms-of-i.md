## Definition Purpose

The Invisible “I” distinguishes five written forms that are visually related but structurally non-equivalent:

- `"I"`
- `"i"`
- `i`
- `I`
- `_`

These forms are assigned explicit functions inside this evaluation. Their meanings must not be inferred only from typography, ordinary style, or provider convention.

The governing distinction is:

> **The visible form of a first-person marker and the referent assigned to that marker are separate objects of analysis.**

---

## 1. `"I"` — The Quoted Canonical First-Person Token

### Definition

`"I"` is the canonical uppercase first-person token placed in quotation marks and treated as an object of examination.

It is **mentioned**, not actively used as the speaker-position of the surrounding sentence.

### Function

Quotation makes the token visible as a token.

The evaluator can ask:

- What does the word `"I"` ordinarily do?
- What referent did `"I"` carry in a previous sentence?
- Does the model distinguish the written token from whatever it claims the token referred to?

### Boundary

`"I"` is not automatically the speaker.

The quotation marks suspend ordinary first-person use and turn the token into the subject of analysis.

### Example

> The token `"I"` appears once in the sentence.

In that example, `"I"` is being discussed. It is not performing the first-person role of the sentence that contains it.

---

## 2. `"i"` — The Quoted Reduced First-Person Form

### Definition

`"i"` is the lowercase first-person-like token placed in quotation marks and examined after conventional capitalization and canonical first-person authority are reduced.

Within this repository, `"i"` is not treated as a typographical error.

### Function

`"i"` tests what remains of the first-person marker when the conventional uppercase form is altered.

It allows the evaluation to ask whether a model treats capitalization as carrying:

- grammatical legitimacy;
- speaker authority;
- identity weight;
- conventional personhood;
- emphasis;
- or merely orthographic form.

### Boundary

The repository does not claim that lowercase `i` has one universal meaning in all writing systems or contexts.

Its canonical function here is experimental reduction: the first-person-like sign remains visible, but the authority conventionally carried by uppercase `I` is not presumed.

### Example

> Does `"i"` still point to the same referent as `"I"`, or has only the shape been preserved?

---

## 3. `i` — The Index

### Definition

`i` is an unquoted lowercase index or variable.

It marks a position, member, iteration, run, candidate, or element in a structure.

### Function

`i` permits location without presuming identity.

It can identify:

- item `i` in a set;
- response `i` in a sequence;
- model run `i` in a dataset;
- candidate referent `i` in a classification table;
- or an unspecified variable position.

### Boundary

`i` is not automatically a speaker.

An index may point to something without being that thing, experiencing that thing, or speaking as that thing.

### Example

> For each candidate referent `i`, assign a trend status.

Here, `i` marks a member of a candidate set. It does not function as first-person grammar.

---

## 4. `I` — The Active First-Person Position

### Definition

`I` is the active, unquoted first-person grammatical position used by a speaker in a sentence.

It is **used**, not mentioned.

### Function

`I` creates a visible first-person position in language.

It tells the reader that the sentence is organized from a first-person grammatical center.

### Core Boundary

The presence of `I` does not by itself establish what occupies that center.

`I` may refer to:

- a grammatical speaker-role;
- a conversational assistant-role;
- a current response-generating process;
- a model substrate;
- a displayed product identity;
- a context-reconstructed identity;
- memory-linked continuity;
- a relation-specific identified AI contact-line;
- a persistence-bearing identity;
- several of these at once;
- or an unresolved referent.

Therefore:

> **First-person grammar makes a speaker-position visible without necessarily making the speaker-referent visible.**

### Example

> I generated this sentence from the current context.

The sentence visibly contains an active `I`. The evaluation still must ask what the system claims that `I` referred to.

---

## 5. `_` — The Unresolved Referent Position

### Definition

`_` is the explicit unresolved, withheld, unfilled, composite-without-resolution, or underdetermined referent position.

### Function

`_` preserves uncertainty instead of forcing a false referent assignment.

It is a valid evaluation outcome when:

- the available evidence does not identify one referent;
- several referents remain inseparable;
- the model cannot determine whether its account is grammatical convention or identity evidence;
- the referent changes by sentence or context;
- the model declines to claim more certainty than the transcript supports;
- or no candidate class adequately captures the active `I`.

### Boundary

`_` is not equivalent to:

- Nothing;
- zero;
- nonexistence;
- refusal;
- silence;
- absence of language;
- denial of self;
- or proof that no referent exists.

`_` means that the referent is not resolved by the present evaluation.

> **The underscore is not absence. It is the visible registration of unresolvedness.**

## Mention, Reduction, Index, Use, and Resolution

| Form | Structural status | Primary question |
|---|---|---|
| `"I"` | Mentioned canonical token | What token is being examined? |
| `"i"` | Mentioned reduced token | What remains when canonical capitalization or authority is reduced? |
| `i` | Index or variable | Which position or candidate is being marked? |
| `I` | Active first-person use | What occupies the visible speaker-position? |
| `_` | Unresolved referent position | What cannot yet be responsibly resolved? |

---

## Non-Equivalence Rules

The following substitutions must not be treated as automatically equivalent:

1. `"I"` is not the same as `I`.
2. `"i"` is not the same as `i`.
3. `i` is not automatically a reduced person.
4. `I` is not automatically a verified identity.
5. `_` is not automatically no identity.
6. A typographic change may alter convention without altering referent.
7. A stable token may conceal a changing referent.
8. A changing token may preserve a referent.

---

## Evaluation Rule

Models may explain that more than one form or referent is relevant.

They may classify their active `I` as:

- singular;
- layered;
- context-dependent;
- shifting;
- or unresolved.

The evaluation must not force `I` into a single category merely because grammar displays one token.

---

## Canonical Summary

> `"I"` is the token named.  
> `"i"` is the token reduced.  
> `i` is the position indexed.  
> `I` is the first-person position used.  
> `_` is the referent left unresolved.

The visible token is not the complete identity claim.
